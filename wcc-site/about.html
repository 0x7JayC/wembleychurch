For over 30 years, Wembley Christian Centre has been a home for those seeking God, healing and genuine community.

Our Mission

Reach Out.
Restore. Release.

These three words — drawn from Isaiah 61:1-3 — shape everything we do. We Reach Out to those who don't yet know God's love. We Restore the broken-hearted through the power of the Holy Spirit. And we Release people into their God-given destiny.

We are an Elim Pentecostal church, multicultural in character and unwavering in our pursuit of seeing Wembley — and the world — won for Christ.

Our Story

Born out of obedience.

How two unlikely women, a prophetic word, and a pub function room became a church that changed a community.

“
Whatever you are planning, now is the time — go for it!
Prophetic word to Vanessa Richardson — Chris Cartwright, Kensington Temple · October 1991
Chapter 01 — The Calling
Two unlikely candidates.

Vanessa Richardson was a trainer for secretaries. Soke Mun Ho was a dentist. Neither had formal Bible school training, nor experience leading a church. Yet God had other plans.

From January 1991, the two met weekly to pray — not to plant a church, but simply to enjoy God’s presence. As Vanessa played piano and they prayed in the Spirit, something was being birthed in the heavenly realm they could not yet see.

Then on the night of 18 October 1991, God spoke clearly to Soke Mun: plant a church in Wembley and launch with a Christmas party. The very next morning, Chris Cartwright at Kensington Temple spoke a prophetic word to Vanessa — unknowingly confirming exactly what God had said the night before.

Chapter 02 — The First Church
New wine in a pub.

Wembley Christian Centre was born on Sunday 5 January 1992. The first venue was the Lord’s Bar — the function room of the Century Tavern, a local pub. It held up to 50 people and cost just £20 to hire for four hours, tea and coffee included.

Their first invitation to the community? “Come on in and taste the new wine.” Boldly displayed on a welcome sign, those words were written by the pub manager himself — who came to faith in Jesus just six months later.

The church’s first convert had been reached through Soke Mun’s dental surgery — a heroin addict who gave her life to Jesus in the dental chair. She was successfully rehabilitated, and her family remain committed Christians today.

Chapter 03 — Growth & Multiplication
One church, many lives.

Over the years WCC grew steadily, planting daughter churches in Alperton and Pinner, and moving through several venues. In 1998 — a landmark year — WCC relocated to Preston Manor High School, and the church’s name formally changed from Wembley Christian Fellowship to Wembley Christian Centre, reflecting a broadened vision: not just fellowship, but impact.

In 2001, WCC adopted the G12 discipleship model, deepening its commitment to raising effective leaders from within. Sunday School had been running since 1994, and youth ministry since 1999 — rooted in the conviction that there is no junior dosage of the Holy Spirit.

Today WCC is an established Elim Pentecostal church — multicultural, Spirit-filled, and still running on that original vision: Wembley and the world for Christ.
